CSCD 618/DSCD 604: Deep Learning and Neural Networks

Course description

Deep learning is an exciting and rapidly advancing field that utilises artificial neural networks to derive deeper insights from data. By employing a set of algorithms and models organised into multiple processing layers, deep learning enables complex data representation and extraction of valuable insights. Deep learning has significantly advanced the state of the art in various fields, including computer vision, speech recognition, natural language processing, and bioinformatics. In this course, we will explore deep neural networks, focusing on architectures such as feed-forward models, which process information in a single direction, and convolutional models, which are tailored for image tasks. We'll also cover essential techniques for sequence modelling, crucial for tasks like language translation and time series analysis. Additionally, we'll examine innovative concepts such as variational and adversarial models for data generation, attention mechanisms that enable models to focus on relevant aspects of input data, and optimisation strategies to improve model performance.

Learning outcomes

At the end of this course, students should be able:

  • To develop an advanced understanding of deep learning and different neural network architectures, such as convolutional neural networks and Transformers, which represent state-of-the-art approaches for today's data-driven predictive modeling.
  • To identify real-world scenarios where application of deep learning makes sense in problem-solving across various fields and application areas.
  • To develop and implement repertoire of different algorithms and approaches in deep learning, and understanding their various strengths and weaknesses.
  • To combine both theoretical and practical concepts to conduct research in deep learning, and communicating the outcome professionally in form of a scientific paper and a formal oral presentation.

Prerequisites

There are no official prerequisites for this course; however, it would be very helpful if the student have proficiency in Python, comfortable with college level Calculus & Linear Algebra (solving basic derivatives and understanding matrix vector operations and notation), and Probability & Statistics (understanding of basic probability, gaussian distributions, mean, standard deviation, etc.)

Final project

The final project offers students the chance to practically apply what they have learned in class to a problem of their interest. The potential projects can be:
Algorithmic track. Develop a new model (algorithm) or a variant of existing models to tackle a problem of your choice (e.g., vision or language problem). This research track may be more challenging; however, it can lead to publishable work in a reputable conference/journal.
Application track. Choose a real-world problem and use existing deep learning models to solve it. If you have a specific background and interests, such as biology, engineering, economics, or physics, we encourage you to apply the deep learning algorithms covered in this class to problems in your domain.

Students responsibilities and conduct

Students are expected to read the assigned materials before each scheduled class, attend and actively participate in discussions, complete assignments and projects on time, and seek help as soon as they encounter difficulties. Attendance will not be taken; therefore, you are expected to attend class regularly and be prepared to participate in the learning process and contribute to your group project. Students are expected to turn off their mobile phones and refrain from sending emails, texts, and other messages. Also, be respectful and courteous towards other students and the instructors.

Academic honesty, collaboration, AI tools usage

UG students and faculty are expected to uphold the highest standards of academic honesty. Students can find information on the core principles and standards in the University's policy on academic integrity, which is accessible at the University of Ghana Plagiarism Policy. Plagiarism includes, but not limited to:

  • Copying of code or text with none or minimal reformatting (e.g., just changing variable names);
  • Just translating an algorithm or a script from one language to another; and
  • Reproducing (copying and pasting) code or text generated by an automatic code/text generation tool.

Students must ensure that full acknowledgement (citation) is given to the authors of any source code or text that is reused. Students' works may be checked using similarity-detection software. At a minimum, violating the plagiarism policy and examination rules will result in a grade of 0 for a particular assignment/quiz. Repeated violations may be referred to Academic Affairs for a more severe penalty. This class offers a collaborative learning experience. Students are expected to discuss ideas with their peers but should write their own solutions independently, without referencing solutions from other students. This encourages students to work independently while sharing ideas on how to test their implementation and evaluate their own work, rather than claiming other students' work as their own, which is a crucial part of integrity in their future careers. Sharing your solutions with another student breaches the University's examination rules. Therefore, students should not make their assignment solutions publicly available, such as posting them on social networks or a public git repository (the final project work can be hosted on a public git repository after submission). Students are permitted to use generative AI tools, such as Gemini, GPT-4, and Co-Pilot, in a manner that is considered acceptable as human collaboration. You are not allowed to ask for solutions or copy code directly, and you should indicate when a generative AI tool is used. Like human collaboration assistance, students are ultimately responsible and accountable for their own work.
